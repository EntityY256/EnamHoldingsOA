Detailed Rationale
Crisil Ratings has assigned its ‘Crisil AAA/Stable’ rating to the proposed non-convertible debentures of Sun Pharmaceutical
Industries Limited (Sun Pharma) and has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’ ratings on the existing bank facilities and
commercial paper programme.
The ratings continue to reflect the leadership position of Sun Pharma in the domestic formulations segment, its strong
presence in the US and other regulated markets with a robust specialty products portfolio and strong financial risk profile.
These strengths are partially offset by exposure to regulatory risk and susceptibility to intense competition and US policy
uncertainties.
Operating performance remained healthy in the first quarter of fiscal 2027, with operating income increasing by around 10%
on-year to Rs 15,300 crore, driven by strong growth in the India formulations and global innovative medicines businesses.
India formulations sales grew 16% on-year to Rs 5,475 crore, while global innovative medicines sales increased 13% to $351
million and constituted around 22% of consolidated sales; this was partly offset by a 10% decline in US formulations sales
amid continued weakness in the generics business. Operating profit increased 2.7% to Rs 4,418 crore, though operating
margin moderated to 29% from 31.1% in the corresponding period of the previous fiscal. Nevertheless, operating profitability
remained healthy, supported by a favourable product mix and increasing contribution from the innovative medicines’ portfolio.
Earlier, Sun Pharma’s consolidated revenue grew 12% on-year in fiscal 2026 driven by healthy growth in the domestic,
emerging and rest-of-the-world markets and ramp-up in the sale of innovative medicine products amid subdued growth in the
US formulations market, and the operating margin was healthy at 30.5% (29.3% in fiscal 2025) supported by a favourable
product mix.
Crisil Ratings expects revenue to grow at a high single-digit rate annually over the medium term, supported by the scale-up of
the innovative medicines portfolio, including the recently launched Leqselvi and UNLOXYCT, and steady growth across other
key markets. Operating margin is expected to remain healthy at 26-27% over this period, notwithstanding sizeable research
and development (R&D) spend of 6-8% of revenue and upfront marketing and commercialisation expenses for innovative
medicines ahead of commensurate revenue scale-up. While Sun Pharma’s agreement with the US administration provides
some mitigation against tariff-related risks for a two-year period, after which the arrangement is subject to review, the evolving
policy environment in the US, particularly implementation of Most Favoured Nation (MFN)-linked drug pricing measures and
tariffs on pharmaceutical products, and their consequent impact on realisations and operating profitability, will remain
monitorable.
Earlier, Sun Pharma had announced on April 27, 2026, the proposed acquisition of US-based Organon & Co. (Organon; rated
‘BB/CreditWatch Positive/A-4’ by S&P Global Ratings) for an enterprise value of $11.75 billion. Organon, a global healthcare
company spun off from Merck, reported revenue of $6.2 billion and adjusted operating profit of around $2 billion in calendar
year 2025, translating into a healthy operating margin of around 31%. During the first half of calendar year 2026, revenue
remained broadly stable on-year over $3 billion, while operating margin sustained around 30%. Established brands accounted
for around 60% of revenue during the period, followed by innovative medicines in women’s health at 27% and biosimilars at
13%.

The acquisition should significantly strengthen Sun Pharma’s global scale and diversify its business profile across therapies,
products and geographies. On consummation, the combined entity is expected to rank among the top 25 global
pharmaceutical companies, with pro-forma revenue of around $12.4 billion. The transaction will provide Sun Pharma with an
established presence in women’s health and biosimilars, expand its geographical reach and further strengthen its innovative
medicines portfolio. On a pro-forma basis, established brands and branded generics are expected to constitute around 51% of
consolidated revenue, followed by innovative medicines at 27%, generics at 15%, biosimilars at 6%, and active
pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) and others at 2%. Operating profitability is expected to remain healthy, with earnings before
interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (Ebitda) margin of 28-30%, translating into operating profit of around $4 billion and
supporting healthy annual cash generation.
Crisil Ratings believes the acquisition provides a strong strategic fit and should strengthen Sun Pharma’s business risk profile,
though the sizeable transaction will result in a temporary increase in leverage. The transaction has received approval from
Organon’s shareholders and is expected to be completed over late 2026 and early 2027, subject to requisite regulatory
approvals across relevant jurisdictions. Integration risks arising from the scale and geographical spread of Organon’s
operations are expected to be mitigated by a structured implementation plan and management oversight. Nevertheless, timely
integration of operations, realisation of envisaged business synergies, and the evolution of contingent liabilities and regulatory
matters will remain key monitorables.
The financial risk profile is expected to moderate temporarily following the acquisition but should remain supported by strong
cash generation and subsequent deleveraging. Sun Pharma is expected to deploy $2.0-2.5 billion of its cash surplus and
internal accruals towards the acquisition, with the balance funded through additional debt of $9.5-10.0 billion. Consequently,
leverage is expected to increase materially immediately following the transaction, with net debt to Ebitda estimated at 1.5-2.0
times in fiscal 2028, the first full year of operations post-acquisition. However, healthy cash accrual generation from the
combined business should support sizeable debt reduction thereafter, consistent with the management’s stated philosophy of
maintaining a debt-light balance sheet.
The financial risk profile remained strong, as reflected in robust adjusted networth of over Rs 71,000 crore as on March 31,
2026, moderate debt (including lease liabilities) of Rs 4,627 crore (Rs 2,362 crore as on March 31, 2025) and sizeable cash
surplus of over Rs 30,000 crore as on March 31, 2026.
Analytical Approach
Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of Sun Pharma and its various subsidiaries, joint ventures
(JVs) and associates, collectively known as Sun Pharma, as these entities have considerable operational and financial
linkages. For JVs and associates, a moderate integration approach is followed — Crisil Ratings factors in the share of profit
from JVs, as also any incremental investment required by them. Intangibles (such as brands and trademarks) and goodwill on
consolidation have also been amortised over five years.

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths
Leadership position in domestic formulations
Sun Pharma is the leader in the domestic formulations segment (contributed 33% to revenue in fiscal 2026) with a market
share of ~8.5% as on June 30, 2026 (source: All India Organisation of Chemists and Druggists MAT Data for June 2026). Its
leading position in the chronic segment is backed by specialisation in technically complex products, strong brand equity and
large product portfolio. Sun Pharma has 31 brands among the top 300 brands in the domestic market. It also ranks among the
top 10 consumer healthcare companies in India and enjoys strong brand equity for key products such as Revital (vitamin and
mineral supplement) and Volini (pain reliever). These brands give it a competitive edge and have helped lay the foundation for
the global over-the-counter (OTC) business. Sun Pharma sells OTC products in over 20 international markets. Its key
therapeutic areas in domestic formulations are neuropsychiatry, cardiology, gastroenterology, pain-analgesics and anti-
infectives. The company continues to enjoy a dominant position in these segments.
Strong presence in the US and other regulated markets with robust specialty products portfolio
The US, which contributed around 27% to revenue in first quarter of fiscal 2027 (29% in fiscal 2026), is among the key
geographies for Sun Pharma. The company ranks 13th among the generic pharmaceutical companies in the US. It has a
robust product pipeline and had filed 677 abbreviated new drug applications (ANDAs) and 70 new drug applications (NDAs) of
which 119 ANDAs and 13 NDAs were pending approval from the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) as on June 30,
2026. With extensive R&D over the past several years, Sun Pharma has established a robust portfolio of innovative medicine
products in the US and other regulated markets. The successful launch and commercialisation of Deuruxolitinib (Leqselvi) in
July 2025 and UNLOXYCT (through Sun Pharma’s acquisition of Checkpoint) in January 2026 is expected to further
strengthen innovative medicine product offerings over the medium term. Healthy market position in the other regulated
markets of Europe, Japan, Canada and Australia is supported by the company’s strength in the innovative medicines, generic
and OTC segments.
Strong financial risk profile
Driven by a healthy adjusted networth and moderate debt, adjusted gearing was low at 0.06 time as on March 31, 2026. Large
cash accrual will suffice to cover moderate organic capital expenditure (capex—including the greenfield capex) and
incremental working capital requirement. Debt protection metrics were healthy with adjusted interest coverage ratio above 50
times for fiscal 2026.

Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses
Exposure to regulatory risk
Sun Pharma faces regulatory risk with instances of adverse observations for its plants. In December 2022, the US FDA issued
an import alert for the company’s facility in Halol, Gujarat. Following this, the company also received a warning letter about its
facilities at Dadra and Mohali and the facility at Baska was classified as Official Action Initiated (OAI) by US FDA. This will
impact sales of the facilities until non-compliances are resolved. Four of its facilities (at Taonsa, Karkhadi, Paonta Sahib and
Dewas) continue to be subject to certain provisions of the consent decree of permanent injunction. Resolution of pending
regulatory issues and sustained compliance remain monitorable. Crisil Ratings notes that Sun Pharma has provided for
sizeable settlement amounts historically and continues to make proportionate provisions factoring the on-going litigations and
tax liabilities under dispute, the impact of which may not be ascertainable at present. Higher-than-expected movement in such
provisions, settlements or any adverse regulatory outcomes materially impacting cash surplus would be monitorable.
